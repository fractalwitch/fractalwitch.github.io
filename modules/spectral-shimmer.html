Anatomy · what makes it this

  1. Moving light pens. Every mark is a pen that draws only its newest piece each frame: ribbons along the drag, rays growing out from a point, sparks flying, rings tracing round, hairs chasing the hand. Nothing is redrawn whole.
  2. The ground is the exposure. Pens add light onto a black film; each frame the film fades by the afterglow amount. Short fade is a live shimmer, long fade is a long exposure, zero is a drawing.
  3. White-hot core, spectral body. Each stroke is a soft wide glow, a coloured body and a thin white core. Hue comes from a colour law: distance travelled, angle, speed, a palette or one laser hue.
  4. Dispersion. Two thin copies offset to either side, shifted down and up the spectrum, split every edge like light through a prism.
  5. Sparkle. Glowing sprites twinkle on flung sparks and on needle tips; a bloom pass blurs the whole exposure back over itself.

Failure modes

  • Redrawing a lasting shape every frame on an additive ground: it piles up to a white donut. Draw it once as a pen, or scale its alpha by the fade (steady brightness = alpha ÷ fade).
  • Fading with a low-alpha fill alone: 8-bit rounding leaves a ghost floor. A tiny difference pass each frame clears it.
  • Hue as one rainbow ramp over the whole canvas: reads as a gradient sticker, not light. Tie hue to the pen's own path, angle or speed.
  • Too many simultaneous wide glows: everything blows out to white. Keep glow passes faint and let overlap make the white.
